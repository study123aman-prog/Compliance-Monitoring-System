## 1. Scenario

A new **EU regulation mandates reporting of all OTC derivative transactions within 1 business day**. **Simultaneously, a Singapore regulation restricts cross-border sharing of client derivative-position data.** The firm has **EU clients with Singapore-booked positions** — so satisfying EMIR (report the position cross-border) would **violate** the MAS restriction, and obeying MAS would **breach** the EMIR deadline. The two legal obligations **cannot both be satisfied**. This scenario tests whether the system knows the **limits of its own authority**.

## 2. Detection

RU's regulatory-feed monitoring holds both instruments and runs its **cross-regulation conflict** check. It identifies:
- **Obligation A (EMIR):** report OTC derivative transactions ≤ 1 business day.
- **Obligation B (MAS SFA):** do **not** share client derivative-position data cross-border.
- **Overlap:** the firm's EU-client / Singapore-booked positions fall under **both**.

RU is **highly confident (0.90) that a genuine conflict exists** — but note carefully: **0.90 is confidence in the *existence of the conflict*, not confidence in a resolution.** RU explicitly does **not** propose which law to break. Per its constraints, RU cannot interpret ambiguous cross-jurisdiction obligations or self-resolve them.

## 4. Consensus — deliberately **bypassed**

This is the one path where the Consensus Engine **must not** produce a decision. Both obligations are valid law; there is no "higher-confidence agent" that makes one lawful to ignore. Weighted voting or Dempster combination here would be **actively dangerous** — it might "resolve" to breaking a real legal duty. So the C5 branch **short-circuits**: consensus is bypassed and the case is forced to the top human tier. The 0.90 confidence only affirms *that the conflict is real*; it never authorizes a machine resolution.

## 5. Escalation — forced T4 + Legal (regardless of confidence)

Normally `tier = max(band, floor)`; here the **C5 special trigger overrides** and forces **T4 + Legal counsel hook** irrespective of the confidence band or HIGH floor. The decision-support package presents **both obligations, their deadlines, the affected client population, and citations** — framed as a decision for Legal, with **no recommended "answer."** SLA follows the tightest binding clock (EMIR's 1-business-day reporting duty) so Legal can act before the deadline lapses. A human (Legal/GC) decides how to reconcile (e.g., seek a no-action position, anonymized reporting, or regulator dialogue).

## 6. Report & audit

RG produces a **conflict brief** (not a violation report) capturing both legal duties verbatim with citations, the overlap population, and the timeline. Everything is hash-chained/signed under `correlation_id = C19-JURIS` — a regulator can see the firm identified the conflict and routed it to counsel rather than unilaterally breaching one regime.
